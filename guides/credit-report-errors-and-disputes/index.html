Guide

Your credit report: how to read it, find errors and dispute them.

Most people have never read theirs line by line. It is not hard, and it is where the single largest obstacle is usually hiding.

In short

  • A report has four parts. Read all four, not just the accounts.
  • Errors that change a payment record or an ownership matter most.
  • You can dispute directly with the bureau, yourself, at no cost.

(01)The anatomy of a report

Four sections, in every report.

The layout differs between the credit bureaus, but the contents do not.

01

Personal information

Your name, addresses, employers. Wrong details here are often the first clue that somebody else’s file has been mixed with yours.

02

Accounts

Every credit account, its limit, its balance, its payment record. This is the bulk of the report and where most errors sit.

03

Public records

Bankruptcies and similar court records where the bureau reports them.

04

Inquiries

Who has looked at your file and when. Applications you made, and reviews you did not ask for.

(02)The errors that matter

Not every mistake is worth your energy.

An old employer listed wrongly costs you nothing. These do.

ErrorWhy it mattersWhat to check
An account that is not yoursIt can carry a balance and a payment record that were never yours to begin with.The account number, the opening date, and whether you recognise the lender at all.
A payment marked late that was paidPayment history carries more weight than anything else on the file.Your own statements and bank records for the month in question.
A balance that was settledA cleared debt still showing as owed distorts both your utilisation and your obligations.The settlement letter or the final statement, and the date it was reported.
A collection you have never heard ofOld bills get sold on. The buyer reports under its own name, not the original one.Who the original creditor was, and whether the debt is even yours.
A duplicate accountThe same debt reported twice counts twice against you.Matching balances and opening dates under slightly different names.
An inquiry you did not authoriseIt signals borrowing you never applied for, and can indicate fraud.The date, and whether you applied for anything in that week.

(03)How to dispute

Five steps, and none of them cost money.

  1. 01

    Get the report

    Obtain your report from the credit bureau. You are entitled to it, and you do not have to buy a subscription to exercise that right.

  2. 02

    Identify the item

    Read line by line and mark exactly what is wrong. One item, one dispute. A vague complaint about the whole file gets a vague answer.

  3. 03

    Document it

    Find the statement, the letter, the receipt. A dispute supported by a document is a different conversation from a dispute that is not.

  4. 04

    File with the bureau

    Dispute with the credit bureau that is reporting it. If more than one is reporting the same error, each one needs its own dispute.

  5. 05

    Follow up

    The Fair Credit Reporting Act gives the bureau a statutory reinvestigation window, set out at 15 U.S.C. 1681i, to respond. Keep what comes back, whatever it says.

(04)Where Smart Start comes in

You do not have to do this alone, and you can still see all of it.

You upload your report and we read it back to you plainly: what is on it, what is hurting most, and what each item is costing you. The strategy, the disputes and the creditor contact are carried out by an attorney affiliated partner organisation.

Every stage is visible in your tracker, and you upload whatever arrives in the post so that the file stays current. We publish no duration for any of it, because the bureaus and the creditors set that clock.
